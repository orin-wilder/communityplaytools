The overlap

You're overlaying the city. So am I — just one totem at a time.

Cityverse's pitch, as I read it, is a trustworthy digital layer over a real, physical St. Petersburg — built for the people who actually live here, not an algorithm optimizing for engagement. I'm not borrowing that thesis for this page. It's the exact idea behind Signal Fire, the product I've been building and running here in St. Pete.

Most people don't skip an unfamiliar gathering because they're not interested. They skip it because nothing tells them they're welcome.

Signal Fire's answer is physical first: a durable QR marker planted at a beach court, a community garden, a park. Scan it and you get a place-specific board — who's really here, what's happening right now, hosted by a real person — before anyone opens an app. Curated, never scraped, on purpose. It's the same argument I'd guess sits under your own thinking about local journalism and community media, just pointed at gatherings instead of news: a small, trustworthy, human-curated signal beats an infinite algorithmic one.

Where AI actually shows up

An AI feature I'd defend in front of your team.

The AI Center of Excellence's whole premise, as I understand it, is helping people, businesses, and governments see it, engage it, and master it when it comes to AI — not just be talked at by it. Signal Fire's Admin Event Scout is my small, real attempt at holding that same standard inside a shipped product.

Scout goes looking for real local events with an AI web search (OpenRouter, routed to Gemini). A human has to say yes before anything publishes. Every AI-sourced event carries a visible provenance label, so nobody mistakes the machine's guess for a host's word. The design principle is simple: AI does the searching, a person owns the judgment call.

How I actually build

Program rigor, civic scar tissue, and an AI-native workflow.

Seven years at Sony PlayStation taught me how to coordinate engineers, legal, and finance under deadlines that don't move — PayPal integration, international tax systems, the PS5 prerelease purchase system, on time, during a console launch. Two years running Beautiful PB in San Diego taught me the civic side: pushing a contested bike boulevard through a parking board that had blocked similar projects before, and raising $450,000 to save a historic building. Different rooms, same skill — too many people with competing priorities, find a way forward.

Now I build the tools myself. Rails and React Native, Supabase for data, and Claude Code as a daily working tool the same way I'd use a linter or a test suite — it clears the mechanical work so I can spend my judgment on the actual product decisions. This site, Signal Fire's codebase, and the Idea Mashup toy above were all built that way.

To be direct about scope: I'm not an ML engineer and I'm not chasing that title. What I am is a full-stack builder who ships fast and is honest about how, and a civic organizer who knows this city's texture — from Williams Park to the Historic Gas Plant District redevelopment I've been following closely.
