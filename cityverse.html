The overlap

You're overlaying the city. So am I, just one totem at a time.

Cityverse's pitch, as I read it, is a trustworthy digital layer over a real, physical St. Petersburg, built for the people who actually live here instead of an algorithm optimizing for engagement. I'm not borrowing that thesis for this page. It's the exact idea behind Signal Fire, the product I've been building and running here in St. Pete.

Most people don't skip an unfamiliar gathering because they're not interested. They skip it because nothing tells them they're welcome.

Signal Fire's answer is physical first: a durable QR marker planted at a beach court, a community garden, a park. Scan it and you get a place-specific board, who's really here, what's happening right now, hosted by a real person, before anyone opens an app. Curated, never scraped, on purpose. It's the same argument, I'd guess, running under a lot of what you're building: a small, trustworthy, human-curated signal beats an infinite algorithmic one. That's the thread I keep seeing between your company's work and mine, using AI and technology to genuinely reinvigorate civic and community life instead of just optimizing for attention.

And I did read past the homepage. A Civic Language Model trained on hyper-local St. Pete data. Interactive experiences putting regular people in the mayor's chair to feel what the tradeoffs actually cost. The line that AI adoption is more cultural than technical, which could be CPT's mission statement with the nouns swapped: play is how a culture adopts anything new, and making adoption feel like play instead of homework is the entire reason my company exists.

Where AI actually shows up

An AI feature I'd defend in front of your team.

The AI Center of Excellence's whole premise, as I understand it, is helping people, businesses, and governments see it, engage it, and master it when it comes to AI, not just be talked at by it. Signal Fire's Admin Event Scout is my small, real attempt at holding that same standard inside a shipped product.

Scout goes looking for real local events with an AI web search (OpenRouter, routed to Gemini). A human has to say yes before anything publishes. Every AI-sourced event carries a visible provenance label, so nobody mistakes the machine's guess for a host's word. The design principle is simple: AI does the searching, a person owns the judgment call.
