Signal Fire

A permission structure for strangers to become participants.

Signal Fire lives next to a chain-link fence at a beach court, on a stake at a community garden, on a post at a park. A durable QR-coded totem opens a place-specific event board in the browser — no app required. You read who hosts here, what's coming up, and whether something is happening right now, then walk in already knowing you're welcome.

It's built on the belief that physical presence creates social permission — and that a small, host-verified set of real gatherings is more trustworthy than a scraped, algorithmic event feed.

Signal Fire · how it works

Three surfaces, one signal.

Orange means now. Across every screen, the one warm signal is reserved for what's happening in the present moment — so it always means something.

🏙️

City Board

The network's homepage at signalfire.live/stpete. A stranger lands here and reads, in order: that the network exists, that it's local, that it's curated, and that they're invited. Place-first — six totems, events nested underneath.

🪧

Totem Board

The per-location board a QR scan opens. A founding-host story panel lowers stranger anxiety before you arrive, upcoming events sit below, and you can check in during the active window.

🧑‍🤝‍🧑

Host Page

A shareable URL for a host's whole gathering portfolio at /h/{host} — built to live in an Instagram bio. The primary call is simple: Follow this host and know when they're next.

Built fullstack on Rails 8.1 + Expo / React Native, PostgreSQL via Supabase, with admin-approved, provenance-labeled event sourcing. Curated, never scraped.
