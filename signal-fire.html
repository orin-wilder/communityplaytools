The founding insight

Most people don't skip an unfamiliar gathering because they lack interest. They skip it because nothing tells them they're welcome.

Signal Fire provides that signal physically, before anyone opens an app. The product lives next to a chain-link fence at a beach court, on a stake at a community garden, on a post at a park.

It has to earn its place in that environment before it earns a place on a screen. Scan the marker, see what's happening here, and walk in already knowing you belong — no prior relationship required.

How it works

Physical first, then a screen.

Signal Fire is a fullstack product with three layers — and the order matters. Presence creates permission; the app only adds what a persistent identity unlocks.

01 · Physical

The totem

A durable, QR-coded marker placed at a recurring gathering spot. Scanning it opens a location-specific event board right in the browser — no download, no account, no friction.

02 · Web

The boards

A Rails application serves the public site, the totem boards, host pages, and a host dashboard — and exposes a clean JSON API for the mobile app to consume.

03 · Mobile

The follow

An Expo / React Native app adds the things that need a persistent identity: favorites, host follows, and a personalized weekly digest so you never miss what's next.

Three surfaces

Place first, then people.

The primary unit is the totem — a place — not the event. Quiet totems still show up; hidden cards aren't acceptable.

Who it's for

Three promises, one network.

🚶

For attendees

Discover recurring, host-verified gatherings at the places you already walk past. Scan a marker, see what's happening, and walk in knowing you're welcome — no prior relationship required.

🔥

For hosts

Get found across your whole gathering portfolio, not just one event. Build a following of people who want to know when you're next — without relying on platforms that don't serve hyperlocal, recurring gatherings.

🌐

For the network

Prove that physical presence creates social permission — and that a small, curated, admin-verified set of real gatherings is more trustworthy and more usable than a scraped, algorithmically ranked feed.

Design language · Civic Beacon

Orange means now.

Ember is reserved for one job: live states, active calls to action, and present-moment affordances. Everywhere else is calm paper and ink — so when the one warm signal appears, it carries weight.

If orange shows up on a screen and isn't pointing at something happening right now — or a button that leads straight to it — it's a bug.

The voice matches the look: plain neighborhood-speak. The test — could it appear on a chalkboard outside a coffee shop and be read sincerely? If not, we rewrite it.

Applied AI · Admin Event Scout

AI that goes looking. A human who says yes.

Signal Fire's curation promise — every gathering hosted by a real, verified person — doesn't scale if admins have to manually hunt for new totems and events. So Scout does the hunting: an admin triggers an AI web search (routed through OpenRouter to Gemini) for real local events near a totem, gets back a shortlist, and reviews it before anything goes live.

Nothing an admin doesn't approve gets published. Every event Scout finds carries a visible provenance label, so a host or attendee can always tell what a person found versus what the AI surfaced first. It's a small feature, but it's the difference between "AI-powered" as a marketing word and AI as one more accountable tool inside a real product.

Product discipline

What we deliberately left out.

What a product refuses to do says as much as what it ships.

✕Scraped or unclaimed events. They dilute the curation promise. Every gathering is hosted by a real, verified person.
✕Reactions. No signal yet supports them, so they stay out until they earn a place.
✕A map on the city board. Deferred to V2 — six totems fit one screen.
✕Filters and search. Unnecessary at this scale; a clean, curated list beats a query box.
